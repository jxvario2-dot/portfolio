My exploration of questions beyond the domain of math and physics.

What is value?

This question came to me in the form of "What makes money valuable?' To answer that question, I thought about what makes money less valuable. I found two things that make money less valuable: the overproduction of money and the loss of the ability to use it. When too much money is printed in any economy, the value of the currency decreases. In other words, the value of money comes partially from its rarity.


The value of currency also decreases if you are unable to use it. A situation like this would happen if, hypothetically, every government were to collapse. In other words, part of a currency's value comes from its potential to be used. A dollar bill just becomes a high quality piece of paper if it can't be used.


However, while these are probably the closest things to objective measures of value, they aren't the only measures of value. After all, if they were, Guerica by Picasso would be no more value than my poor attempts at art from middle school. This lends to the idea that subjective value exists. In fact, I believe all value is subjective to an extent. Going back to the previous measures of value that we examined, we can use rarity as an example. Rarity is dependent upon a frame of reference. For example, on earth, helium is relatively rare and valuable. However, on the scale of the universe, helium is astonishingly common, making up 24% of the mass in the universe.


To conclude, value is a subjective thing no matter how you look at it. However, some ways of measuring it can get very close to being objective, which is why we've clung on to the idea of things having value.

Life

There are so many philisophical questions that can be asked regarding life. I will not claim to know the answers to them, but I will state my beliefs regarding them. I am a stern believer that all life has value, whether it be a worm, a human, a mushroom, a tree, or algae on a lake; it all has value. This belief is based on my previous assertion that value comes from rarity. Yes, on earth, life is extremely common. Yet, on the scale of the universe, it is one of the rarest things that we know of. This gives life significant value. But what is the purpose of life? For most species, it comes down to the survival of both the species and the individual to spread genes. But I believe humans have gotten to a point at which we can choose our own purposes. Therefore, we don't have inherent purposes.
